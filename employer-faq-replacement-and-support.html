Replacement, contract and support questions

When something goes wrong

What happens if a selected worker does not join?

It happens, and the honest reason is usually that something in the offer was not what he was told. We tell you why, and the replacement position is whatever your written agreement with us says — ask for those terms before you commit, not after.

What happens if a worker fails the medical test?

He does not travel, and nothing about that is negotiable in any GCC state. He is replaced from the tested pool and the medical result stays confidential to him. Build a small margin into your numbers, because a proportion of medical failures is normal in every batch.

What happens if a worker fails the trade test?

He never reaches your shortlist. That is the entire point of testing before selection rather than after arrival — the failure happens in New Delhi at our cost, not on your site at yours.

What happens if a worker is unsuitable after joining?

Tell us early and tell us specifically — what he cannot do, or what he will not do. Those are different problems with different answers. The replacement position is set out in your written agreement, and we would rather have stated it plainly at the start.

Do you offer a replacement?

Ask us for our replacement terms in writing before you commit. We would rather state them plainly than let you assume something we have not agreed — and an agency that promises replacement without defining the period or the conditions has not promised you anything.

What is your replacement period?

It is a term of the written service agreement, agreed before you commit, and you should read it together with the refund terms. We will not quote a period on a web page that may not match the agreement you actually sign.

How quickly can you send a replacement?

The tested pool is what makes a replacement fast — the next man is already assessed, so sourcing is not the delay. The delay is the same as the first time: visa and permit. Tell us immediately rather than at the end of the month.

Contract models and who employs the worker

Do you recruit for short-term contracts?

Yes, and shutdown and turnaround work is exactly that. Be aware that a short contract changes who applies: the man who accepts six months is often not the man who would accept two years, and we will tell you how that affects the shortlist.

Do you recruit for long-term contracts?

Yes, and two-year contracts are the common pattern for GCC work. Longer contracts attract men who intend to stay, which is what you want — provided the offer is realistic enough to hold them for the full term.

Do you recruit for project-based contracts?

Yes. Tell us the project duration and whether there is work after it, because the candidate will ask and an honest answer at interview stage is worth more than a comfortable one.

Do you provide temporary staffing?

We recruit men onto your contract, for whatever term that contract runs. We are not a staffing body that keeps workers on its own books in your country, and that distinction matters for who the legal employer is.

Do you provide permanent recruitment?

Yes, in the sense that applies to the GCC: direct recruitment onto your own employment contract and your own sponsorship, renewable as you choose. That is the standard arrangement for every requirement we handle.

Do you provide outsourced manpower?

No. We do not hold workers on our own books in your country and we do not act as a labour supply company inside the GCC. The men are recruited onto your sponsorship, which is a different model and a cleaner one for compliance.

Who will be the legal employer of the worker?

You are. The worker is recruited onto your employment contract and your sponsorship in the country of work. Neither TKVK nor Taj HR Services becomes the employer at any point.

Who manages payroll and worker benefits?

You do, as the legal employer, under the labour law of the country of work. Our part ends at arrival and the handover of a complete, briefed, documented worker.

Who manages leave, insurance and labour compliance?

You do, in your own country and under its law. We will tell you what the worker was promised at interview, because a dispute about leave or benefits almost always begins with a gap between the briefing and the contract.

Agreements and day-to-day support

Can you provide a sample service agreement?

Yes, on request to a company email. Read it before you send a demand letter rather than after, because the payment, replacement and refund terms are all in it and they are easier to discuss before anyone has spent money.

Can you provide a sample employment contract?

Yes, as a reference format consistent with the labour law of the destination country. It is a starting point for your own legal review, not a substitute for it — we are not your legal advisers and will not pretend to be.

Who will be our single point of contact?

One named person for the whole requirement, from the demand letter to arrival. Reaching a different person every time is how details are lost, and the detail that gets lost is usually the one in the candidate's briefing.

Can you provide daily or weekly recruitment updates?

Weekly as standard, daily during mobilisation or a shutdown window. The update says what moved, what did not and what is waiting on your side — including when the answer is that nothing moved this week.

How do you handle worker complaints, absence or early resignation?

Tell us as soon as it starts rather than when it is final. Most early resignations trace back to a gap between what the man was told and what he found — accommodation, overtime or the actual job. We will talk to him, tell you honestly what he says, and tell you if the gap was ours.
