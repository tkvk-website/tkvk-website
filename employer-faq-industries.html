The industries and projects we recruit for

Twelve questions about sectors and project types. The useful answers here are the qualified ones — offshore work and shutdown deadlines in particular, where the honest answer has a condition attached to it.

Sector decides the gang, not just the trade

The same trade behaves differently by sector. A mason on a Qatari project judged against a sample panel, a mechanic diagnosing a fault in a running plant, and a welder on a fixed-date shutdown are three different hiring problems even before the test starts.

Tell us the sector and the project type with your requirement and we will propose a gang mix against it — including where you are better off with fewer skilled men and more helpers, which is a conversation most agencies avoid.

Industry and project questions

Do you recruit for construction projects?

Yes, and construction is the largest part of what we test for: masons, carpenters, steel fixers, scaffolders, welders, pipe fitters, electricians, plumbers and the helpers who work alongside them. Tell us the package and we will propose the gang mix rather than one trade at a time.

Do you recruit for oil and gas projects?

Yes, for the trade-level roles: welders, pipe fitters, scaffolders, electricians, mechanics and HVAC technicians. Oil and gas sites usually set their own test standard, so send yours and we will test to it rather than to ours.

Do you recruit for shutdown and turnaround projects?

Yes. Shutdown work is the hardest kind of requirement because the date is fixed and the numbers are large. Give us the window early — mobilisation, not testing, is what decides whether men are on site for day one.

Do you recruit for maintenance projects?

Yes, and maintenance work needs a different man from new construction. Diagnosing a fault in a running plant and working around occupants are behaviour questions as much as skill ones, and the assessment comments on both.

Do you recruit for manufacturing and factories?

Yes — welders, mechanics, electricians, forklift operators, packing helpers and warehouse staff are the usual factory mix. Repetitive-process discipline is tested separately from trade skill, because a factory needs consistency more than peak output.

Do you recruit for logistics and warehousing?

Yes: warehouse and logistics staff, packing helpers, forklift operators, heavy and light drivers and delivery riders. Where the role needs a licence as well as hands, the licence status is stated for each man before you select.

Do you recruit for hotels and hospitality?

Yes, hospitality staff is a tested category — kitchen helpers, stewarding, service and housekeeping roles, male only. Language and presentation are assessed alongside the work, because in hospitality the guest sees both.

Do you recruit for retail and customer service?

Yes, mall and retail staff is a tested category, male only. Spoken English, counting accuracy and conduct with customers are what the assessment looks at, and we report the English level honestly rather than as a blanket claim.

Do you recruit for facility-management contracts?

Yes, and FM contracts are almost always a mix of categories rather than one. Send us the scope and the headcount per role and we will propose the mix, including where you are better off with fewer skilled men and more helpers.

Do you recruit for infrastructure projects?

Yes — steel fixers, masons, carpenters, scaffolders, heavy equipment operators and drivers are the usual infrastructure requirement. Long remote postings are normal on this work, and we brief the candidate on that before he signs, not after.

Can you supply workers for remote sites and labour camps?

Yes, and we tell the candidate the location and the accommodation arrangement at interview stage. A man who accepts a remote posting knowing the facts stays; one who finds out after arrival is the man who leaves, and that costs you more than the visa.

Can you supply workers for offshore or marine projects?

Offshore work usually needs sea survival and offshore safety certification that we do not issue and most of our pool does not hold. For yard, port and marine shore-side work, yes. For genuinely offshore postings, tell us the certificates required and we will say plainly whether we can meet them.
