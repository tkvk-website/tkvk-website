Why household staff are tested before they reach your home

Household hiring in the UAE is more regulated than most employers expect. Domestic worker contracts run through the Tadbeer system, and the terms are written down — which is good for everybody, and still says nothing about whether the man can cook.

That gap is where households get disappointed. The paperwork is handled and the person arrives, and only then does anyone discover he has never used an oven. We close that gap before he flies.

The recruitment licence is held by Taj HR Services — B-3252/DEL/PER/1000+/5/11251/2025, issued by India’s Ministry of External Affairs — and every deployment to the UAE runs through it.

What we assess

  1. A cooking test to your household's cuisine

    Say what your family actually eats and the man cooks it under assessment. Photographs of the food go with the profile. We do not write “good cook” as an opinion.

  2. Villa routine with other staff

    Most UAE villa roles sit alongside a driver, a nanny or a gardener. We assess whether a man works with others without friction, because a household with staff conflict is a household that replaces everybody.

  3. Family driver assessment where included

    Road test, licence class verified, and conduct with passengers including children. Where the role is driving-first we say plainly whether he is ready for Dubai traffic or not.

  4. Discretion and privacy

    Handling belongings, guests and what is said in the house. This is assessed as behaviour during preparation rather than promised in a profile.

What we require before we place anyone

We place men only in household roles, and we place them only where four things are in the written contract: the wage and how it is paid, the working hours and the weekly rest day, the accommodation and food, and that the worker keeps his own passport. These are not requests. A household that will not put them in writing is one we decline, because the alternative is a man stranded four thousand kilometres from home and a problem that eventually becomes yours too.

Papers and what we will not promise

Domestic placements in the UAE run through the Tadbeer framework, and employment documents for this category need attestation by the Indian Mission. Every man also clears an approved medical, police clearance and eMigrate PoE clearance where his passport requires it. Tell us the route your household or agent normally uses and we brief the man on it before he flies.

Household work carries no trade certificate, so what you receive is the assessment itself — what he cooked, how he drove, what he completed unsupervised — along with the work history we could verify and a plain note where we could not.

What households ask us

Do you supply housemaids or nannies?

No. TKVK supplies male workers only — cooks, houseboys, family drivers and gardeners. We say so at the start rather than take a requirement we cannot honestly fill.

Can he cook Indian and Arabic food both?

Some can and many cannot, and we test rather than assume. Tell us both cuisines matter and the assessment covers both, with photographs of what he actually produced.

Our household has young children. Does that change the assessment?

For a driver it changes it considerably — child seats, school runs, patience. Tell us and we weight the assessment and the briefing accordingly.

What do you require from us before placement?

A written contract covering wage and how it is paid, hours and the weekly rest day, accommodation and food, and that the man keeps his passport. If those four are not in writing we do not proceed.
