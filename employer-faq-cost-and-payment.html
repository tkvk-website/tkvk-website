Cost, salary and payment

Nineteen questions about money. You will not find a price list below, and that is deliberate: a figure printed on a web page is not a figure you can hold anyone to. What you will find is who carries which cost and what to ask for in writing.

Send your requirement All employer questions

Why there is no price list here

A recruitment cost depends on the trade, the numbers, the country, the salary you are offering and the scope you want us to carry. Any agency that gives you one figure before knowing those five things is either guessing or leaving something out of it.

What you should insist on, from us or from anyone, is a written quotation that lists inclusions and exclusions separately, states the basis of the fee, and sets out the payment, replacement and refund terms. Compare that document between agencies — not the headline number.

Cost, salary and payment questions

What it costs and how it is quoted

What is the salary expectation for each role?

Salary is set by you, not by us. What we do is tell you honestly whether the figure you are offering will attract and hold the standard of man you are describing, in that country, for that trade. An unrealistic offer does not fail at the interview; it fails three months after arrival.

What is the basic salary for each category?

Send us the basic you intend to offer per category and we will respond on each one. We do not publish a salary table, because a number on a web page would be out of date and would tell you nothing about your own site, country and contract terms.

What is the overtime rate?

Overtime is governed by the labour law of the country of work and by your employment contract, not by us. State the rate in the contract and we will make sure the candidate is briefed on it before he signs rather than surprised by it after arrival.

What is the total recruitment cost per worker?

It is quoted in writing against your actual requirement — trade, numbers, country and scope — not taken from a published price list. Ask for the quotation before you commit to anything, and compare it against what is included and excluded rather than the headline figure.

What is included in your recruitment fee?

That is set out item by item in the written quotation and service agreement, so that you can see exactly what you are paying for. Ask for it in that form before you commit — a fee described in a conversation is not a fee you can hold anyone to.

What is excluded from your recruitment fee?

Exclusions are listed as explicitly as inclusions in the written quotation, and they are the part worth reading twice. Government charges, employer-side permit costs and anything payable in your own country are the usual exclusions in this industry.

Is the fee charged per worker, per month or per project?

The basis is stated in the service agreement before you sign it. Ask for it to be spelled out — per worker, per batch or per project — because the basis matters more than the number when the requirement changes mid-way.

Who pays for what

Who pays for visa and work-permit costs?

The employer. In every GCC state the work permit and employment visa are issued to the employer or sponsor, and that cost sits on your side by the structure of the system, not by agreement. India's emigration rules also cap what a recruiting agent may collect from a worker.

Who pays for medical tests and document attestation?

Stated in the service agreement. Both are real, invoiced third-party costs — approved medical centres and attestation authorities — so whoever carries them, they should appear as named line items rather than be folded invisibly into one figure.

Who pays for flight tickets?

A commercial term, agreed in writing before mobilisation. Most GCC employment contracts place the outbound ticket on the employer; whatever you decide, it must be in the contract the candidate signs so that he is not told one thing and charged another.

Who pays for accommodation?

Your contract decides it, and it must be explicit. Accommodation provided, accommodation allowance, or neither — whichever it is, the candidate is briefed on it before departure, because this is the single commonest cause of a worker leaving in the first months.

Who pays for food and transport?

Set in your contract and briefed to the candidate before he signs. Food allowance and site transport vary widely between GCC employers, so stating them plainly in the offer is worth more than a generous but vague description.

Who pays for medical insurance?

The employer, in practice and by law in the GCC states that mandate health cover for employees. The scope of that cover is worth stating in the contract, because what is covered differs far more than whether cover exists.

Who pays overtime?

The employer, under the labour law of the country of work. The rate and the method of calculation belong in the employment contract, and the candidate is briefed on both before he signs.

Who pays end-of-service benefits?

The employer, under the labour law of the country of work. It is a statutory entitlement in every GCC state rather than a negotiable term, and it is not something a recruitment agency carries on your behalf.

Payment terms

What are your payment terms?

Stated in the service agreement before you sign, and we would rather state them plainly than let you assume something we have not agreed. Ask for them in writing at the quotation stage, not after the first batch is tested.

Do you require an advance payment?

That is part of the payment terms agreed in writing with you. Whatever the structure, you are entitled to see it set out before you commit — and so is any employer dealing with any agency.

What is your refund policy?

Ask for the refund and replacement terms in writing before you commit, and read them against the replacement period. We would rather set them out plainly at the start than argue about an assumption later.

Can you provide a formal quotation?

Yes. Send the requirement — trade, numbers, country, salary and start date — and you receive a written quotation with inclusions, exclusions, payment terms and the replacement position set out. That document, not a conversation, is what you should be comparing between agencies.
