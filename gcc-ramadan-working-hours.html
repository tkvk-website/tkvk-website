Ramadan working hours in the six Gulf countries

For one month a year, every Gulf country shortens the working day. The cut is different in each country, and in some it applies to every worker while in others it applies only to Muslim employees. If your crews are mixed, or you run sites in more than one country, here are the rules side by side.

When Ramadan falls

Ramadan follows the lunar calendar and moves about eleven days earlier each year. In 2026 it ran from around 19 February to 20 March. For 2027, astronomers expect it to begin around 8 February, with Eid al-Fitr around 9 or 10 March. The exact days are fixed by moon sighting, and Eid al-Fitr brings paid public holidays across the region.

Because it now falls in late winter, Ramadan no longer overlaps the summer midday work ban. For several years the two will hit different parts of the calendar, which matters if you are planning a programme across the whole year.

What it means for your manpower plan

Mixed crews work different hours in some countries

In Saudi Arabia, Oman and Bahrain the reduced hours are set for Muslim employees. A crew of Muslim and non-Muslim workers on the same site can end up on different schedules, so plan supervision and handovers for both.

Output drops for about a month

With two to three hours off the day, a project that needs fixed output in February and March will need either more men or a longer timeline. Decide which before sending a requirement, because it changes the headcount.

Avoid arrivals in the last days of Ramadan

Offices, medical centres and government services slow down around Eid. Men who land in the last week of Ramadan often wait until after the holiday for medicals, residence permits and site induction. Arrivals a few weeks before Ramadan, or just after Eid, usually start work faster.

Common questions

Do Ramadan working hours apply to non-Muslim workers?

It depends on the country. Onshore UAE cuts two hours a day for all private-sector employees, and Qatar and Kuwait cap the week at 36 hours regardless of religion. In Saudi Arabia, Oman and Bahrain the reduced hours are set for Muslim employees.

How many hours can employees work in Saudi Arabia during Ramadan?

Muslim employees in the Saudi private sector work a maximum of six hours a day or 36 hours a week during Ramadan.

How much are working hours reduced in the UAE during Ramadan?

Under the UAE labour law, normal private-sector working hours are reduced by two hours a day during Ramadan. Employers may use flexible or remote arrangements within the reduced hours. The ADGM and DIFC free zones have their own rules.

When is Ramadan in 2027?

Astronomical projections put the start of Ramadan 2027 around 8 February, with Eid al-Fitr around 9 or 10 March. The dates are confirmed by moon sighting, so final dates are announced only a day or so in advance.

Can an employer ask for overtime during Ramadan?

Overtime is governed by each country's general labour law and the employment contract; the Ramadan rules reduce normal hours rather than ban extra work. Check the specific country's rules and the contract before planning it.
