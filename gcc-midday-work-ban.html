The GCC summer midday work ban, country by country

Every Gulf state stops outdoor work under the midday sun in summer, but the hours, dates and penalties differ. If you run sites in more than one country, or are planning a summer mobilisation, the differences change how many men you need. Here are the 2026 rules side by side, with sources.

What it means for your manpower plan

Qatar and Kuwait lose the most hours

A five-hour stop, plus travel to and from camp, leaves a short productive day for outdoor crews. Contractors usually split the shift — an early start and an evening return — or add men to hold output. Decide which before you send a requirement, because the answer changes the headcount.

Plan for the hardest month, not the easiest

June and the first half of September are covered in some countries and not in others. If a project spans two countries, or runs from May into September, size the crew for the period with the longest stop.

Outdoor trades take the hit; indoor trades do not

Steel fixers, scaffolders, shuttering carpenters and masons work in the sun and lose the full window. Electricians, plumbers and HVAC technicians on internal fit-out, and facility or hospitality staff, mostly do not. A summer mobilisation can lean towards the indoor trades and bring the outdoor crews in for the cooler months.

Exempt work still needs protection

Where an exemption applies — for example emergency repairs to water, power or telecoms in the UAE, or oil and gas and continuous utilities in Bahrain — employers must still provide shaded rest areas, cold drinking water and first aid. Bahrain also requires a daily working-hours schedule posted at each site.

Common questions

Which Gulf country has the longest midday work ban?

Qatar and Kuwait. Qatar stops outdoor work from 10:00 to 15:30 between 1 June and 15 September. Kuwait stops it from 11:00 to 16:00 between 1 June and 31 August. Both take five or more hours out of the working day.

When does the Saudi midday work ban start?

Saudi Arabia bans work under direct sun from 12:00 to 15:00 for private-sector establishments, from 15 June to 15 September, under the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development.

What is the fine for breaking the UAE midday break?

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation fines Dh5,000 per worker, up to Dh50,000 where several workers are involved, and can take further administrative action.

Does the ban apply to indoor work?

No. Every country's rule covers work in open areas exposed to direct sunlight. Shaded, indoor and air-conditioned work continues, which is why indoor trades such as finishing, MEP fit-out and facility work lose fewer hours.

Are there exemptions?

Yes, but they are narrow. In the UAE they cover work that cannot technically be interrupted, such as emergency repairs to water, power or telecoms, and some traffic-critical public works. Bahrain exempts oil and gas, continuous utilities, security and rescue services, notified emergency maintenance and approved government projects. Exempt sites must still provide shade, water and first aid.
