Why accommodation helpers are assessed before shortlisting

Facility contracts in the UAE are measured. Response times, complaint counts and audit scores decide whether a contract survives its first year, and every one of those numbers is produced by the men on the floors.

High-rise work also has its own rhythm: lifts, service corridors, bin rooms and residents at all hours. A man who is good in a low-rise villa compound is not automatically good in a forty-storey tower, and we say which one we are sending.

The recruitment licence is held by Taj HR Services — B-3252/DEL/PER/1000+/5/11251/2025, issued by India’s Ministry of External Affairs — and every deployment to the UAE runs through it.

What the preparation covers

  1. Cleaning method and chemical safety

    Dilution, colour coding and product separation. We record who arrived knowing it and who had to be taught, because that difference shows up in your first audit.

  2. High-rise routine and service-lift discipline

    Working floors in sequence, using service routes rather than resident lifts, and not leaving equipment in a corridor. Most tower complaints are about this, not about cleanliness.

  3. Conduct and complaint handling

    Knocking, privacy, and how he behaves when a resident complains at him directly. A man who argues once costs you more than a month of good cleaning.

  4. Pace against a measured round

    A timed round of a set area, measured twice in the session. Facility contracts run on minutes per unit, so we give you the number rather than the word “fast”.

Papers and what we will not promise

There is no meaningful certificate for accommodation cleaning, so we send the preparation record instead — method, chemical handling, timed rounds and conduct observations — with the work history we verified and a clear note on what we could not.

Alongside the licence, every candidate’s passport and ECR status, approved medical, police clearance and eMigrate PoE clearance are sequenced so nothing expires while your visa is in process.

What employers ask about building helpers

We are measured on response time. Can you assess for that?

Tell us the standard and we time the man against it rather than describing him as quick. You get two timings from the session, not the flattering one.

Do they know colour-coded cleaning systems?

Some do; most learn it in preparation, and we record which is which. We will not describe a man as trained in a system he met for the first time last week.

Can they work night shifts in accommodation blocks?

Many can, and we tell the men plainly in the briefing when the work is nights. A man who learns his shift on arrival is a man who asks to go home.

What about English for resident interaction?

We record the actual level rather than claiming fluency. Most handle simple instructions and basic courtesy; if your towers need more, say so and we filter for it.
