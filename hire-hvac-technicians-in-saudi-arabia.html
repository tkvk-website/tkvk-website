Why AC and HVAC technicians are tested before shortlisting

In Saudi Arabia air conditioning is not a comfort trade, it is a continuity trade. When a chiller drops in July the building does not become uncomfortable, it becomes unusable, and the cost lands on your maintenance contract within hours. That is why we test diagnosis rather than installation: an installer who cannot fault-find leaves you a backlog and a client complaint.

The plant is also getting larger. District cooling and central chilled-water systems are increasingly normal in new Saudi developments, and a plant room is a different world from a rooftop package unit — pumps, valves, water treatment, BMS points and a fault that can sit anywhere in the loop. A good split-unit man is not automatically a plant-room man, and we will tell you which one you are looking at.

Heat is the third factor. Rooftop and plant-room work in a Saudi summer is genuinely hard, and a technician who has only worked Indian conditions has not met it. We brief honestly on that before he signs, because the men who leave in the first season almost always leave because nobody told them.

What the trade test covers

  1. Fault-finding on a live unit

    He is given a unit with a fault and asked to find it. How he narrows it down tells you far more than any certificate.

  2. Gas handling and pressure work

    Charging, recovery, leak detection and gauge reading, and whether he works cleanly with refrigerant rather than venting it.

  3. Brazing and pipework

    Joint quality on copper, insulation and support — the work that decides whether a system leaks in year two rather than year ten.

  4. Electrical and controls

    Contactors, capacitors, thermostats and control wiring. A technician who cannot test the electrical side is half a technician.

  5. Chiller and plant-room exposure

    Whether he has genuinely worked on central plant — pumps, valves, water side and BMS points — or only on unitary equipment. We do not blur the two.

Refrigerants: what we actually check

One thing worth asking every candidate, and worth us testing: which refrigerants he has actually worked on. A man whose whole career was R-22 is not automatically safe on R-410A or R-32. The operating pressures are higher, the oil is different — POE rather than mineral — so contamination and moisture matter far more, and R-32 is an A2L, mildly flammable, which changes how he brazes, recovers and stores it. Gulf fleets are mixed: old R-22 units still running alongside newer R-410A and R-32 equipment, often in the same building. We record which refrigerants each man has genuine hands-on time with, and we say plainly where he has none, because "AC technician" on a CV tells you nothing about this.

Certificates and papers

ITI Refrigeration and Air Conditioning certificates and any prior Gulf service record are verified and sent with the profile. Installation experience and maintenance experience are stated separately, because they are different jobs and employers keep being sold one as the other. Passport and ECR status, approved medical, police clearance and eMigrate PoE clearance are prepared so the file clears the Saudi visa process first time.

What employers ask about AC and HVAC technicians

Do you supply chiller technicians or only split and package men?

Both, but not interchangeably. Ask for chiller specifically and we will send men who have genuinely worked central plant. Where someone has only unitary experience we say so rather than letting the word "HVAC" cover it.

Can they handle a BMS-controlled system?

Tell us the system. Some men have real BMS exposure and most have none, and we would rather tell you that now than have you discover it on site. Basic controls we do test.

Do you supply women technicians?

No. TKVK supplies male workers only. If your requirement needs female staff we tell you that at the start rather than take the order and try to fill it.

Can you mobilise a maintenance team in bulk?

Tell us the number and the contract start date. We give you a schedule built on your visa quota and attestation position, and we say plainly when a date will not hold.
